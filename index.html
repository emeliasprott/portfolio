The Economics of AI Reliance

Using task-level data from the Anthropic Economic Index, this research applies credence-goods theory to estimate where people rely on AI for work they may not be able to evaluate independently.

After the Crash:
Housing Recovery in the Bay Area

A neighborhood-level analysis on the long-term effects of the 2008 housing crash on the housing market in the Bay Area. Using federal mortgages, census, and regional economic data, I illustrate some of its lasting impacts on homeownership and displacement.

  • Economic trends

Platform Enforcement and Misinformation on Twitter

Using synthetic difference-in-differences, this project estimates how a six-day wave of Twitter account suspensions changed misinformation sharing. The design addresses the unusual problem that enforcement itself removes some treated accounts from the observed post-suspension data.

  • Synthetic difference-in-differences
  • Causal inference
  • Experiment design

Understanding the whole system.

In my opinion, data makes more sense when understood within in terms of the system that generated it. Data science provides the technical foundation for my work, and political economy shapes how I frame questions and interpret evidence.

I pay close attention to the relationships and incentives behind a dataset, then use that context to build analysis that is both methodologically sound and practically useful. I am most drawn to work where the difficult part is not only technical execution, but deciding what question to ask and what the available evidence can reasonably support.

When I'm not looking for work, you can find me taking a year to make a quilt, falling asleep a few pages into a book, or being bossed around by a chihuahua named Garfunkel.
